1. Introduction

    The aviation industry is regarded as one of the primary sources of global carbon and air pollutant emissions (Chen et al., 2016). In
particular, near-surface emissions from airport operations can affect both the environment and the health of populations (Bo et al.,
2019; Eastham and Barrett, 2016; Hudda et al., 2020; Quadros et al., 2020). The air quality can be worsened by flight departure
activities of hub airports, such as Zurich Airport (Switzerland) (Zhang et al., 2020), Kansai Airport (Japan) (Baxter et al., 2020),

Hongqiao and Wuhan Airport (China) (Xu et al., 2023).
    Increasing flight operation tasks leads to higher flight delays at the airport field and low-altitude flight route congestion during the
take-off phase, especially at hub airports. Flight delays increase the taxiing and waiting time of aircraft at the airport, while flight route
congestion increases the time aircraft spend in the air circling, generating excessive near-surface CO2 emissions (Grewe et al., 2021).
This environmental concern, as well as the air traffic delay caused by the congestion at airports, calls for the need to optimize the
departure schedule of flights (Benlic, 2018; Katsigiannis and Zografos, 2021; Pellegrini et al., 2017; Tan et al., 2022; Wang et al.,
2023).
    On the other hand, the development of the aviation industry also introduces cooperation among airports in the same region. The
multiple airport region (MAR) with a core airport (usually the hub airport) and multiple airports operating cooperatively is becoming
emerging worldwide, such as the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei MAR (BTH-MAR) in China, the New York MAR in the United States and the
London MAR in the United Kingdom. Although MARs effectively release the burden of hub airports, the MAR needs to share the same
flight route resources, and coordination among airports within the MAR is required to improve the operational efficiency of the
airspace.
    Given the nature of the data and computation requirements for the cooperation optimization of the flight departure among several
airports, the digital twin technology, which can facilitate the mapping and real-time interaction between virtual and reality, can be
well suited to the dynamic and complex operating environment of airports. In addition, the real-time data transmission and interaction
capabilities of DT can be used in data-sharing among airports (Souanef et al., 2023), supporting real-time optimization in the complex
aviation network (Negri et al., 2020).
    However, the abovementioned formulations mostly apply a static flight schedule, which does not consider the random fluctuation
of the flight schedule due to the congestion of the flight route and the runway (Guimarans and Padrón, 2022), while dynamic pro­
gramming can hardly solve the problem in a real-time manner due to the complexity of computation and the size of data. In addition,
most of the studies focus on operational efficiency, while the environmental offsets have been seldom quantified. And that’s where the
DT method strengths come in.
    In addition, the common optimization model for flight scheduling considers airport operation capacity only (Zografos et al., 2012).
Some studies have introduced delay time constraints, including the maximum displacement slots of each flight and the number of
adjusted flights (Zografos et al., 2016). The solving algorithms of the current optimization studies for the flight scheduling problem
include linear programming solvers, heuristic algorithms, and reinforcement learning models (Wu et al., 2023). These optimization
methods are also far less effective than the introduction of DT methods in terms of real-time and speed of processing complex data.
    From the research scope perspective, most of the previous scheduling optimization models for airport flight resources have focused
on the airport itself, while ignoring the interactions among surrounding airports, especially within the same MAR, where airports are
supposed to cooperate. Although some researchers have considered cooperative relationships between airports, competition for
airspace between neighboring airports is overlooked (Mannino et al., 2021; Rosenthal and Eisenstein, 2016). The constraints of shared
airspace within MAR require real-time data-sharing among airports, which further loads the burden of the computation of dynamic
programming.
    In this work, an airport departure flight scheduling optimization model is proposed and solved in a DT-facilitated system to reduce
the total CO2 emissions generated from flight delays and departure congestion at hub airports. The proposed model considers the
constraint of airspace resources among multiple airports within the MAR. With the application of DT, the model can simultaneously
optimize departure flight schedules and improve the operational efficiency of airports at the same time. In addition to the operational
efficiency, we also quantified the environmental benefits by comparing the emission reduction before and after the optimization. To
the best of our knowledge, it is the frontier research in the aviation network that co-optimizes the operational and environmental
benefits within a MAR through real-time simulation.
    We selected the Beijing-Tianjin-Hebei MAR (BTH-MAR), one of the most prominent and representative MARs in mainland China, as
a case study to examine the effectiveness of the proposed optimization facilitated by the DT simulation. Specifically, we focus on three
of the busiest hub airports within this MAR: Beijing Capital International Airport (airport code abbreviation: ZBAA), Tianjin Binhai
(airport code: ZBTJ) and Shijiazhuang Zhengding International Airport (airport code: ZBSJ). The exceptionally high flight volume at
these airports, high disparity of flight resources, and complex flight scheduling constraints and stringent handover points capacity
limitations, makes BTH-MAR an ideal example for demonstrating the applicability of the proposed optimization method. Using BTH-
MAR as an example, the optimizations with and without the DT simulation are compared, revealing the necessity of the real-time data
synchronization. Through the case analysis, the emission reduction effects through flight departure optimization are examined. The
impact of the constraints on the operational and environmental efficiency of different levels of hub airports in China during the takeoff
phase is illustrated. The analysis is of general applicability, which is useful for the future development and operational management of
similar airports.
    In summary, the contribution of our study is depicted threefold: 1) we proposed an operational optimization focusing on the flight
departure phase within an entire MAR, instead of a single airport to improve the integration and operational efficiency of MAR; 2) a DT
management system is applied to facilitate the real-time communication among different airports in the MAR, which enhances the
application of the proposed operational optimization facing higher uncertainties; 3) the applicability of the model is proved by using
one of the most complicated MARs in the world, BTH-MAR. The resulting operational strategies provide insightful guidance for the
future improvement regarding the operation and the construction of the MAR to improve both operational and environmental
efficiencies.

2. Optimization formulation and solving algorithm design with digital twin

   This study proposed a flight departure schedule optimization of hub airports within the MAR to reduce the emissions of airport
operation, and applied DT simulation to facilitate the data synchronization among airports. The effects of the optimization are
quantified with both operational efficiency (delay) and environmental (CO2 emissions) measures.

2.1. Departure flight scheduling optimization model

   The proposed optimization model aims to reduce operational delays at airports by reallocating departing flights and alleviating
congestion on departure routes, while considering resource constraints across multiple airports within the MAR. The objective function
focuses on delay reduction, while CO2 emissions can be simultaneously reduced by decreasing taxiing and idling at departure.
Therefore, both operational efficiency and environmental efficiency can be obtained.

2.1.1. Objective function
   The objective of this study is to minimize the total CO2 emissions resulting from flight delays across the MAR. Specifically,
emissions due to unplanned taxiing and waiting times during aircraft field operations are treated as flight delay emissions.
   The CO2 emissions due to flight taxiing during flight delays were calculated based on the Boeing Fuel Flow Method2 (BFFM2)
developed by the International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO), which is calculated as Eq. (2) (Wan et al., 2023).

2.2. Solving algorithm with the DT simulation

   The branch-and-bound method was selected as the solving algorithm for the optimization proposed in this study given its feature of
NP-hard. The description of the branch-and-bound can be found in the Supplemental Information.
   Flight departure scheduling is a dynamic process, which asks for interaction and real-time data transmission among airports. DT
technology creates a virtual space that dynamically reflects airspace utilization and flight conditions in real-time, supporting the
optimization by implementing the algorithm with the latest information.
   In this study, we use Total Airspace and Aerodrome Modeler (TAAM) to create a virtual environment simulating departure flights
before and after optimization under realistic airport operation scenarios. TAAM is a professional simulation tool that models airport
and surrounding airspace operations by reconstructing real-world conditions, enabling quantitative planning, analysis, evaluation,
optimization, and validation of airspace and airport systems. Integrated within the DT framework, the system captures real-time flight
operations at each airport, allowing real-time optimization. A comparative analysis between the conventional branch-and-bound
method and the DT-facilitated approach is provided in Supplemental Information, with case details outlined in Section 3.

    According to the duration of time slots (5 min) in this paper, in total of 156 + 1 = 157 flight slots (T) in the analyzed 13 h are set.

the BTH-MAR has five departure handover points. According to the historical data we adopted in this paper, the handover limit for

4. Results in the case domain and sensitivity analysis of optimization parameters

4.1. Operational and environmental efficiency

    The optimization results of departure flight schedule are presented in Fig. 1. Prior to optimization, the departure schedules at each
airport frequently exceeded operational capacity, particularly around 08:00, 14:00, and 20:00. These peak time slots resulted in an
excessive number of flights surpassing the handover point capacity, potentially causing delays and reducing the overall airspace
utilization efficiency within the BTH-MAR. In contrast, the optimized schedule (Fig. 1(b)) ensures that the number of departures at
each airport remains within their respective capacities, and the total number of departure flights within the MAR during each slot
remains within the overall handover point capacity.
    The improvement in operational efficiency from the optimization is shown in Table 3. The optimization smooths the flight
scheduling over time, elevating the operational efficiency of the airports and airspace, and both flight delays and flight route con­
gestions at hub airports are significantly alleviated. Among them, the largest improvement in efficiency was achieved by ZBAA: 139
delayed flights were able to depart on time, which represents 19.9 % of the total number of ZBAA flights. The average delay time per
flight was also reduced by 88.8 s. While the reduction of delayed flights in ZBTJ and ZBSJ is not as obvious as ZBAA due to airspace
constraints (i.e., handover point limitations), the average delay time and average congestion time of flights can be significantly
reduced by more than 30 s and 20 s, respectively.
    Emission reduction is calculated as the difference between pre- and post-optimization emissions, with taxi emissions computed
using Eq. (2) and departure emissions using Eq. (14). The total emission reduction is computed using Eq. (13), which sums the taxi and
departure emission reductions. As shown in Table 4, emissions can be reduced at all selected airports within the BTH-MAR following
the optimization. Among them, ZBAA achieves the largest CO2 emission reduction of 273.59 tons, accounting for 9.9 % of the CO2
emissions from the landing and taking off cycle of all flights at ZBAA on a single day. These results prove the effectiveness of the
scheduling optimization from both operational and environmental perspectives.

4.2. Sensitivity analysis of optimization parameters on emission reductions

   This section analyzes the sensitivity of the results regarding the change of three parameters: airport runway capacity, handover
point capacity, and the maximum delay allowed, as they may be further improved through airport expansion.

4.2.1. Runway capacity
    The results shown in Fig. 2 show a diminishing marginal improvement in the emission reduction as the increase of runways for
three airports due to restricted handover points in the airspace. ZBAA shows the lowest potential of emission reduction by adding new
runways, which is expected to reduce an additional airport CO2 emission of 0.8 % by adding two runways, illustrating limited cost-
effectiveness.
    In contrast, expanding the runways at both ZBTJ and ZBSJ leads to substantial reductions in airport emissions. The optimal number
of runways considering the emissions is 3 and 4 for ZBTJ and ZBSJ, respectively.

4.2.2. Handover point capacity
    Like runway capacity, handover point capacity can be enhanced through the construction of new radar stations. Fig. 3 shows that
more airport CO2 emissions can be reduced as the handover point capacity increases. When the handover point capacity is increased
from 8 to 9, the CO2 emission reduction rises significantly by 2 %, while the marginal CO2 reduction is minimal as the handover point
capacity continues to expand. In the case of BTH-MAR, for example, it is perfectly reasonable to increase the handover capacity to 9,
which would effectively reduce emissions from flight departures.
    In summary, this study finds that increasing handover point capacity can reduce emissions, especially when it approaches runway
capacity. However, given the short duration of peak slots in the daily operation, a cost-effectiveness analysis of such capacity upgrade
is required.

4.2.3. Maximum delay allowed
    This study assumes a uniform maximum delay allowed for all flights within the same airport, while it does not consider the varying
acceptance levels of different airlines regarding the setting of the parameter. Therefore, this section sets a varied level of maximum
delay allowed and compares the results derived. Since ZBSJ achieves an optimal solution with a maximum delay allowed of 1, this
section only compares the effect on ZBAA and ZBTJ.
    We observe a contrasting impact on the two airports in Fig. 4. As the maximum flight scheduling adjustments increases, emission
reduction at ZBAA decreases to 9.52 % and subsequently increases to 9.9 %. Conversely, the emission reduction at ZBTJ first rises to
4.00 % and then declines to 2.67 %.
    This difference can be explained by the balance between the flight delay rate and the individual delay time of each flight. The
average flight delay and the delay rate with the change of maximum delay allowed are listed in the Supplemental Information for
reference. In particular, the results of the emission reduction changes of ZBTJ show that the optimal value of the maximum delay
allowed at this airport is 4. When the maximum delay allowed is less than 4, the average delay time of flights is shorter, but the flight
delay rate is higher. On the contrary, when the maximum delay allowed is more than 4, the flight delay rate is reduced, but the average

5. Discussion and conclusion

    The allocation of departure flight resources is a key strategy for improving operational and environmental efficiency in air traffic
due to its invisible impact on infrastructure, scheduling, and travelers’ demand. Although traditional optimization methods such as
particle swarm optimization, genetic algorithm, and simulated annealing are commonly used in flight scheduling optimization, real-
time performance can hardly be incorporated, and a local optimum can be obtained when handling large datasets. The introduction of
DT optimization offers significant improvements in both algorithm adaptability and data integration compared to traditional
algorithms.
    Our study introduces digital-twin technology to address the problem of airport flight departure among several airports with the aim
of reducing total airport emissions. By simulating and optimizing departure flights from the three BTH-MAR hub airports, the opti­
mization facilitated by DT improves the efficiency of air transport operations. In the case of Beijing Capital Airport, for example, the
average delay time of flights has dropped by 89 s, the flight delay rate has been reduced by 20 %, and the emissions of departure flights
from BTH-MAR hub airports can be reduced by more than 306 tons in a single day. The proposed DT-facilitated framework provides a
feasible solution for future air traffic optimization that incorporates real-time operational data into the solving algorithm. The result of
our study provides suggestions on both infrastructure and operation of airports to elevate the environmental benefits of air traffic.
    While our study focuses on departing flights, the impact of arrival flights should also be analyzed in the future with the incor­
poration between airports and airlines. The effect of taxiway intersections and runway interrelationships on the operational efficiency
of airports is considered as future exploration. In addition, the scope of our study is limited to the operational phase specifically during
flight departures. Future investigations can expand the scope to the entire life cycle for the investigation of the upstream emissions
from airport construction, aircraft fuel usage cycle, and maintenance.
